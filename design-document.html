MFA Under Siege
Design & Strategy

A scenario-based, interactive security training experience designed using Action Mapping methodology to change real employee behavior — not just check a compliance box.

01

Executive Summary

MFA Under Siege is an interactive, web-based security training simulation that puts learners in the shoes of "Alex," an employee facing three escalating MFA attack scenarios — each modeled after real breaches at Uber, Twilio, and Cisco.

Unlike traditional click-through compliance training, this experience uses branching scenarios with real consequences, timed decision-making, and gamification mechanics to create emotional engagement and lasting behavioral change.

Key Differentiator

This training is a live, interactive web application — not a slideshow. Learners experience attack pressure through timed decisions, realistic UI mockups (phone notifications, Slack messages, phone calls), and immediate consequences. The medium IS the message.

02

Business Goal (Action Mapping Step 1)

Using Cathy Moore's Action Mapping methodology, we start with the measurable business goal — not learning objectives.

Business Goal

Reduce MFA-related account compromises to zero by ensuring 100% of employees can recognize and correctly respond to the three most common MFA attack vectors: unsolicited push notifications, MFA fatigue/push bombing, and voice-based social engineering (vishing).

Why MFA Attacks Matter Now

  • MFA fatigue attacks increased 300%+ between 2022-2024 (Mandiant)
  • The Uber breach (2022) — caused by a single MFA push approval — cost an estimated $30M+ in incident response
  • Social engineering is now the #1 initial access vector in enterprise breaches (Verizon DBIR 2024)
  • Traditional security awareness training has a demonstrably low retention rate — scenario-based training shows 4x better recall (SANS Institute)
03

Target Actions (Action Mapping Step 2)

Instead of listing what learners should "know," we define what they should do when faced with real threats.

Attack Vector Target Behavior Scenario
Unsolicited MFA Push Deny the request AND report it to IT Security immediately Scenario 1
MFA Fatigue / Push Bombing Deny all requests, report the push bombing, AND flag any related social engineering messages Scenario 2
Voice Social Engineering Hang up and contact IT through an independently verified channel Scenario 3

The Critical Insight

In all three scenarios, the optimal action shares a common pattern: deny the request + independently verify through official channels. The training reinforces this pattern through repetition across escalating contexts, building the mental model that transfers to novel situations.

04

Learning Experience Design (Action Mapping Step 3)

Each scenario is a practice activity that puts learners into a realistic context where they must perform the target action under conditions that mirror reality.

Scenario 1: The Innocent Push

Difficulty: Easy. A single unexpected MFA notification. Tests the fundamental behavior: don't approve what you didn't request.

Scenario 2: The Pressure Play

Difficulty: Medium. Push bombing + Slack social engineering. Tests behavior under pressure and ability to recognize coordinated attacks.

Scenario 3: The Social Engineer

Difficulty: Hard. A phone call from "IT Support." Tests the hardest skill: resisting a live, persuasive human (or AI voice) requesting MFA approval.

Design Principles

  • Progressive difficulty — each scenario adds complexity and psychological pressure
  • Realistic context — UI mockups of real tools (phone notifications, Slack, phone calls) create immersion
  • Immediate consequences — learners see the impact of their choice instantly, not in a quiz result
  • Three-choice architecture — wrong/partial/optimal choices mirror real decision granularity
  • Real-world anchoring — every scenario maps to an actual breach, making the threat concrete
05

Gamification Strategy

Gamification mechanics are not decorative — each one serves a specific learning purpose.

Mechanic Implementation Learning Purpose
Security Score 0-100 points, starts at 50. Optimal: +20/25/30, Wrong: -20/25/30, Partial: +5/10 Creates stakes and emotional investment in decisions
Timer Pressure 30/25/20 second countdown per scenario (decreasing) Mirrors real attack psychology — attackers create urgency to prevent careful thinking
Breach Cards Real company breach case study shown after each scenario Concrete proof that these attacks happen to real companies with real consequences
Final Report Card Personalized assessment with rating, scenario breakdown, and key takeaways Summarizes performance and reinforces correct behaviors

Why Timer Pressure Works

The timer isn't just a game mechanic — it's pedagogically intentional. Real MFA attacks succeed because they create urgency. By putting learners under time pressure during training, we prepare them for the emotional state they'll experience during an actual attack. If the timer runs out, the worst outcome occurs automatically — teaching that hesitation defaults to compromise.

AI Integration Opportunities

AI tools can enhance both the production and personalization of this training at scale.

Scenario Generation

Claude / ChatGPT — Generate unlimited scenario variations from a template structure. Each run-through can present different contexts, names, and details while testing the same target behaviors.

Voice Synthesis

ElevenLabs — Generate realistic AI voice clips for the phone call scenario, making the social engineering attempt feel disturbingly real. Multiple voice profiles increase variety.

Video Narration

Synthesia / HeyGen — Create AI avatar intro/outro videos that contextualize the training and deliver the key takeaways with a human presence.

Adaptive Difficulty

Custom AI Logic — Adjust scenario difficulty based on learner performance. Got Scenario 1 right? Skip to harder content. Failed? Add a tutorial scenario first.

AI-Enhanced Personalization Pipeline

  • Pre-training: AI analyzes the learner's role (engineer, executive, HR) and generates role-specific scenarios
  • During training: Adaptive engine adjusts timer, complexity, and hint availability based on performance
  • Post-training: AI generates personalized follow-up micro-lessons based on specific mistakes made
  • At scale: LLMs generate fresh scenario content weekly, preventing the "I've seen this before" effect

Technical Architecture

Current Implementation

Layer Technology Rationale
Frontend Vanilla HTML/CSS/JavaScript Zero dependencies = instant loading, no build step, maximum portability
Styling CSS Custom Properties + Animations Dark theme with CSS variables for easy theming; pure CSS mockups reduce asset loading
Hosting GitHub Pages (static) Free, instant deployment, shareable URL, no server needed
State In-memory JavaScript Simple game state (score, choices, current scenario) — no persistence needed for MVP

Enterprise Deployment Options

  • SCORM wrapping: Package as SCORM 1.2 or 2004 for LMS deployment (Articulate, Cornerstone, etc.)
  • xAPI integration: Send granular event data (choice selected, time to decision, score) to an LRS
  • SSO integration: Add SAML/OIDC for enterprise authentication and user tracking
  • API backend: Add a lightweight API for storing results, generating reports, and managing scenario content
09

Scalability

The scenario engine is designed to be content-agnostic. The same architecture supports any security awareness topic.

Phishing Simulation

Email mockups with suspicious links, attachments, and spoofed sender addresses. Teach email inspection habits.

Vishing (Voice Phishing)

Extended phone scenarios with AI-generated voice. Progressive social engineering with escalating manipulation tactics.

Deepfake Detection

Video call scenarios with AI-generated faces. Teach employees to verify identity through secure channels.

BEC (Business Email Compromise)

Executive impersonation scenarios. Urgent wire transfer requests, vendor payment changes, gift card scams.

Content Expansion Model

Each new topic follows the same formula:

  • Define 3 escalating scenarios based on real breaches
  • Map each to specific target behaviors (Action Mapping)
  • Build realistic UI mockups (email client, video call, etc.)
  • Include real-world breach case studies for credibility
  • Plug into the existing scoring and reporting engine

Measurement Framework

Measuring training effectiveness requires tracking both learning metrics and business outcomes.

Learning Metrics (Kirkpatrick Levels 1-2)

Metric What It Measures Target
Completion rate Engagement / relevance > 90% (vs. ~60% for traditional)
Average security score Knowledge acquisition > 75 on first attempt
Scenario-level accuracy Specific skill gaps > 80% optimal choice per scenario
Time to decision Confidence / automaticity < 15 seconds average by retry
Retry rate Intrinsic motivation > 30% voluntary retry
